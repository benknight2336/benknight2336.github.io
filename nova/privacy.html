Privacy Policy

Last updated October 6, 2026

Nova is a desktop app that runs entirely on the user's own computer. It has no servers, collects no analytics, and shows no ads. This policy explains what Nova does with the information it can access.

What Nova accesses

When you choose to connect an account (for example with "Sign in with Google" or "Sign in with Microsoft"), Nova can access:

  • Email (Gmail, or Outlook through IMAP and SMTP): to show your messages, sort them, and send mail you write or approve.
  • Calendar (Google Calendar, or Microsoft Outlook calendar): to show your events in Nova.
  • Basic profile (your name and email address): to label the account in Nova.

Where your data goes

  • Your mail, calendar events, and sign-in tokens are stored only on your own computer. Sign-in tokens and passwords are encrypted with your operating system's protected storage.
  • Nova talks directly from your computer to your email and calendar provider (such as Google or Microsoft). There is no Nova server in between, and the developer never receives your data.
  • Nova's AI features (summaries, sorting, suggested replies, and answering questions about your mail) use a language model that runs locally on your computer. Your mail is not sent to any AI service.
  • Nova does not sell, share, or transfer your data to anyone, and does not use it for advertising.

Google user data

Nova's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Google data is used only to provide Nova's mail and calendar features to you, is not transferred to others, is not used for advertising, and is not read by humans.

Keeping and deleting data

Data stays on your computer until you remove it. Removing an account in Nova deletes its stored mail and sign-in tokens from your computer. You can also revoke Nova's access at any time:

  • Google: myaccount.google.com/permissions
  • Microsoft: account.live.com/consent/Manage

Other services

If you use Nova's optional weather widget, only your city name, or your location rounded to about 1 km, is sent to Open-Meteo to get the forecast. Web pages you open in Nova's built-in browser are loaded from those websites like in any browser.
